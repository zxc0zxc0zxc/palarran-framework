# Market: Buffer and Airbnb

## Buffer: from intent to the first payment

[Joel Gascoigne's report](https://buffer.com/resources/idea-to-paying-customers-in-7-weeks-how-we-did-it/), dated 2011-02-16, describes a small landing page, testing interest in pricing plans, an MVP and the first paying user four days after launch. This is a historical founder account; early clicks indicated intent, not purchases. Test size/representativeness is insufficient for current market sizing.

Transfer: distinguish discovery, pricing intent and completed payment; propose the cheapest honest test before a large build and measure transitions with denominators. A research skill only plans the test and cannot claim it ran. Historical Twitter/HN channels are not automatically assigned to the current business.

## Airbnb: a narrow market and manual validation

In [The Airbnbs](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/the-airbnbs/), dated 2020-12-10, Paul Graham, who worked with the company early on, describes the founders' persistence and growth during YC. This is a retrospective investor account with interests and survivorship bias, not an experiment log.

Transfer: check a specific local need and supply/demand constraints before global market sizing. Manual contact may produce qualitative evidence; it does not establish scalable CAC. Customer messages, travel and publishing are outside a research invocation.

## Transfer check

Trend data complements these methods: it shows search interest, while demand/willingness to pay requires other observations. Output: segments, geography, negative signals, competing workarounds, triangulation and proposed validation. Do not start with a large TAM before defining the buyer unit.
