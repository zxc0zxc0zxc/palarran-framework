# Alternatives: GitLab and Buffer

[GitLab competitor evaluations](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product/ux/competitor-evaluations/) distinguishes evaluating a solution for a specific job from comparing companies. Transfer: common tasks and scenarios for every alternative, capability states with evidence rather than arbitrary feature bingo. This is a published practice; its sales contribution is undisclosed. Do not transfer enterprise scope/cost to an early MVP.

[Buffer annual report 2023](https://buffer.com/shareholders/2023) describes pricing/packaging changes and choosing to serve beginning customers. Transfer: compare the chosen segment, billing unit, onboarding cost and limitations as well as features. This is a management account; do not attribute all subsequent growth to packaging changes without separate verification. The quantitative positioning effect in this case is not reproduced here.

For a startup wedge, a specific underserved job matters more than catching up with an entire platform. Include a direct competitor, an indirect substitute and “change nothing/manual process.” Usually 3–5 relevant alternatives suffice; explain lower coverage in a narrow niche rather than adding irrelevant brands to meet a count. Date pricing snapshots and verify geography/tax/billing period.

Transfer check: candidate wedge → why a buyer switches → switching costs → minimum parity → measurable test. Competitor popularity and individual complaints do not prove an open niche. Screenshot inference is not a hands-on test. Contract: [compare](../compare.md).
